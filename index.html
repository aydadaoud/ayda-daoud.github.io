FIG. 01 — THE HUMAN BEHIND THE CAD

I've never really been good at sticking to just one thing.

I'm a mechanical engineering student at the American University of Beirut, but engineering is only one part of how I see the world. I've always loved the arts: writing, reading, drawing, singing and dancing. Creativity has always been part of me, and it naturally finds its way into the way I approach engineering.

I love taking an idea that exists only in my head and figuring out how to make it real. Give me a workshop, a set of tools, a prototype and a problem to solve, and I'm happy. I enjoy brainstorming, experimenting, getting things wrong, adjusting the design and trying again.

I'm persistent, curious and very much a hands-on person. I care about the people I work with, the projects I take on and the things I commit to. I want to keep learning, building and pushing myself into new challenges.

I don't want to choose between being an artist and being an engineer. I want to bring both into everything I do.

MECHANISM STUDY / 001
MECH 332Completed

Automated Screw-Cap Mechanism

Designed a mechanism to automate the gripping, vertical movement and rotation required to screw a cap onto a container. The design combined a slider-crank arrangement with motorized cap rotation.

  • Developed the mechanism in SolidWorks and used Motion Simulation.
  • Used Python for kinematics and motion analysis.
  • Worked through 20+ design iterations to resolve interference, gripper geometry and simulation contact issues.
  • Reported approximately 2.75 cap rotations, 11 mm vertical travel and 4 mm thread pitch.
  • Reported vertical approach speed of approximately 0.045 m/s.

20+ iterations. Worth every single one.

AUTOMATION / 002
SMART SYSTEMSCompleted

Smart Home Automation

Built and debugged a simulated smart-home system using Arduino components and Tinkercad Circuits. The system monitored environmental conditions and automated several household functions.

  • Monitored energy consumption, light intensity and indoor/outdoor temperature.
  • Implemented automated load shedding.
  • Designed automated curtains and ventilation control.
  • Fan activation logic used a 3°C indoor-to-outdoor temperature difference.
  • Included an LED warning triggered by a fire-alert tone.
  • Tested and debugged the simulation until the system functioned.
